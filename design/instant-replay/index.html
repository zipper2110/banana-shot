Flow

  1. Pause the video on the moment. Click the replay icon at the right end of the playback bar (or X, or the replay icon on a row).
  2. The replay editor opens in a popup window with its own video player. The Points tab stays as it is behind it. A new replay starts 1.5 s before the playhead and ends 1.5 s after it.
  3. Drag the part edges in the bar under the video. Drag or resize the lime frame on the video to zoom. Set the speed, the repeat count and the sound on the right.
  4. A point can have more than one replay. Each replay has a tab at the top of the panel. Add makes one more replay at the playhead.
  5. Preview replay plays the result. Save keeps all replays of the point and closes the popup.
  6. A point with replays has a lime REPLAY tag in the list. On the timeline, each replay is a lime block with spiky edges after its point. The block width shows the time that the replay adds.

Decided on 2026-10-22 (backlog B-50)

  • A point can have more than one replay.
  • The part can start up to 3 s before the point and end up to 3 s after it.
  • The zoom goes in smoothly at the start and out smoothly at the end.
  • The sound slows down with the video by default. An option mutes it.
  • The Highlights export includes the replays of each point that it includes.
  • On the timeline, a replay is lime, and its width is in proportion to the time it adds. It has no text and small spiky edges on all four sides. A narrow replay (whole-match view, about 3 px) is a plain lime tick; the spikes show only when the timeline is zoomed in.
  • The editor opens in a separate popup window, not in the main window.

Assumptions in this mockup (not decided)

  • The replays of one point play one after the other, in the order of their start times.
  • The timeline ruler shows the times of the source video. It skips over the replay segments.
  • The popup is modal: the main window waits until Save or Cancel.

Technical fit

  • The popup needs a second mpv player. The main player pauses while the popup is open.
  • The zoom frame can use the same mouse logic as the crop frame of the Transform tab (CropEditorOverlay), drawn with libass.
  • X, I and O are free keys now.
