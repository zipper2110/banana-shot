# Marketing strategy: the first users

Written on 2026-10-08, two days after the release of 1.0.0. The source is the
author's handwritten notes (three goals: attract users, increase the reach,
expose the app) and a review of the app, the site, and the market.

The numbers in this file are estimates, not measurements. Replace them with
real numbers from the cockpit when you have them.

## 1. Summary

- The first 10 users come from people, not from channels. Ask people that you
  know or can find by name. Do not wait for strangers to find the site.
- The best first users are players who already record their matches and post
  them on YouTube with no editing. They have the problem today.
- The strongest offer is "I edit one of your matches for you, free". The
  result shows the value better than a text.
- Each exported video is an advertisement. The scoreboard already has the
  "BananaShot app" credit line. Add the site link to the video description
  each time.
- Use one main message: "Your full match, without the dead time, with a real
  scoreboard. Free, on your PC, no AI to fix."
- Do not spend money on ads now. Do not open many social accounts now.
- Padel, languages, macOS, and pickleball increase the reach later. Do them
  after the first users show what they want.

## 2. Where we are now

### What helps adoption

- It is free, with no account, no ads, and no watermark.
- It solves one clear problem: a 2-hour recording becomes a 30-minute video
  with a score.
- The competitors are mostly phone apps with AI and a subscription.
  SwingVision Pro costs about $180 per year. Aero AI costs about $179 per
  year. BananaShot costs nothing.
- The video stays on the PC. Many players do not want to upload their
  matches to a cloud service.
- The author is a real tennis player and made the app for personal use. This is a
  good story for communities.
- The site is ready: demos, FAQ, privacy notice, and two real match videos.
- The cockpit already counts site visits, downloads, sessions, and exports.
- The scoreboard shows the "BananaShot app" credit line. It is on by default.

### Big files: an advantage of local editing

- A 2-hour match in 4K 60fps is about 50 GB. An iPhone records about
  440 MB per minute in 4K 60fps (HEVC).
- BananaShot opens such a file from the disk. There is no upload and no
  wait.
- A cloud tool must upload the file first. The time depends on the upload
  speed of the connection:

  | Upload speed | Time to upload 50 GB |
  |---|---|
  | 10 Mbit/s | about 11 hours |
  | 20 Mbit/s | about 5.5 hours |
  | 50 Mbit/s | about 2.2 hours |
  | 100 Mbit/s | about 1.1 hours |

- Phone editors must also play and cut a 4K 60fps file on a phone. A PC
  with a graphics card does this more easily.
- The numbers above are arithmetic, not measurements of a competitor. Do
  not say that a specific app has a file size limit or is slow before you
  test it.
- Measured on 2026-10-08: a 50 GB 4K 60fps file opens in about 1 second.
  The home page and the FAQ say this now.

### What blocks adoption

- Windows only. Many video people use a Mac. Many players use only a phone.
- Windows SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC". Some people stop
  there.
- Each version works for 6 months. Some people can think that this is a trap
  before a paid version. The FAQ explains it, but a first-time visitor does
  not read the FAQ.
- Manual marking takes time. A 2-hour match can take 20 to 40 minutes of
  marking (estimate). The "No AI" message must say that this is a choice for
  accuracy, not a missing feature.
- Nobody knows the app yet. There are no reviews, no mentions, and no
  social proof.

### Competitors and alternatives

| Alternative | What it does | Weak point that BananaShot fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing (upload the raw video) | Free, no work | Nobody watches 2 hours of ball pickups |
| General editors (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere, CapCut, Shotcut) | Cut anything | Long learning curve, no tennis score, the scoreboard is manual work for each point |
| SwingVision (iPhone, iPad) | AI cuts, stats, line calls | About $180 per year, Apple only, AI errors |
| Aero AI, Perfect Swing (iPhone) | AI cuts dead time | Subscription or Apple only, no scoreboard |
| Scorify, BT Tennis Camera, Scoreboard apps | Score on the video while you record | You must keep score during the match. No dead-time cut |
| Tennis Studio (Windows, Microsoft Store) | Cut, crop, score overlay from a Garmin watch | Closest competitor. Check its price and quality before you compare |
| Club camera systems (for example Rackety, TiePlayer) | Automatic recording at the club | Only at partner clubs, the video is on their server |

Action: install Tennis Studio and try it with one match. Write down one
thing that BananaShot does better. Use it in replies when somebody asks
"why not X?".

## 3. Positioning

### The one-sentence pitch

"BananaShot turns your full tennis match recording into a short video with a
real scoreboard. Free, on your PC, and no AI to fix."

### Three supporting points

1. Watchable: only the points, with the score, the stats, and your comments.
2. Correct: you mark the points, so each cut and each score is right.
3. Yours: free, no account, no upload, works offline.
4. Made for big files: a 4K 60fps recording of a full match (about 50 GB)
   opens at once, because there is nothing to upload.

### Words to use and words not to use

- Use: "full match", "dead time", "scoreboard", "share with your friends",
  "your coach", "your opponents", "free", "4K 60fps", "no upload".
- Do not lead with "No AI". Lead with the result. Use "No AI" as the answer
  to "why must I mark the points myself?".
- Do not use "manual" alone. It sounds slow. Use "you decide each cut, with
  one key".

## 4. User groups (portraits)

Your notes list reasons why a person edits the video by hand: not satisfied
with AI, does not want to share the videos, wants control, likes the
process. These are motives. A person can have more than one. The groups below
are situations. Each group has its own place to find the people and its own
message.

### Group A: the club player who records matches (main group)

- Who: an amateur player, often 25 to 55 years old, NTRP 3.0 to 5.0. Plays
  league matches or matches with friends. Has a phone tripod or an action
  camera.
- Problem: has many raw match files. Never watches them again. Friends do not
  watch them.
- Motive from the notes: wants control, does not want to upload, likes the
  process, does not want to pay a subscription.
- Where: r/10s, Talk Tennis forum (tennis-warehouse.com), local Facebook
  groups, WhatsApp and Telegram chats of clubs and leagues, YouTube comments
  of match videos.
- Message: "Turn the match that you recorded last weekend into a 30-minute
  video with a scoreboard. Send it to your opponent."
- Size: large. This group is the main target for the first 100 users.

### Group B: the amateur YouTuber who posts matches (multiplier)

- Who: a player with a small YouTube channel (about 100 to 50,000
  subscribers) who posts full matches. Many of these videos have no score
  and keep all the dead time.
- Problem: editing takes hours, or the videos get few views because they are
  long.
- Why important: each of their videos shows the "BananaShot app" line to all
  their viewers. One YouTuber can bring more users than one Reddit post.
- Where: YouTube search, for example "full match 4.0", "USTA league match",
  "NTRP 3.5 singles", "club tennis match", "tennis match vlog".
- Message: "I edited one of your matches with my free app. Here is the
  result. You can use it, and you can make the next one yourself."
- Many of them record in 4K 60fps with an action camera or a good phone.
  The big-file advantage matters most to this group: "Your 4K 60fps files
  open at once. Nothing to upload."

### Group C: the coach and the academy

- Who: a coach who records students in matches. A small academy.
- Problem: wants to show a student the important points with the score and
  comments. Has no time to edit.
- Why important: one coach can bring many students and parents.
- Features that matter: comments on the video, favorites export, statistics.
- Where: local clubs (start in Batumi and Tbilisi), coach groups on
  Facebook, LinkedIn, coach forums.
- Message: "Mark the key points, add your comments, and send the student a
  short video with the score."

### Group D: the parent of a junior player

- Who: a parent who records tournament matches of a child.
- Problem: wants to send a short video to the family, to the coach, or to a
  college recruiter (USA).
- Where: junior tennis Facebook groups, academy chats, tournament venues.
- Message: "Send the coach only the points of yesterday's match, with the
  score."
- Note: this group is large in the USA. The college recruiting video is a
  strong need.

### Group E: the person who does not trust AI or the cloud

- Who: a person in any group above who tried SwingVision or a similar app and
  did not like the errors, the price, or the upload.
- Where: threads that compare tennis apps, SwingVision reviews and
  complaints, "SwingVision alternative" searches.
- Message: "No subscription, no upload, no wrong cuts."

### Group F: players of other sports that count points (later)

- Padel: grows fast in Europe, Latin America, and the Middle East. The score
  rules are almost the same as tennis. The app supports padel since B-14
  (2026-10-10). The improvements are B-45.
- Pickleball: very large in the USA. It has a different score system. The
  "plain points" mode works now, but a real pickleball score is better.
- Table tennis, badminton, squash, beach volleyball: "plain points" works
  now.
- Do not promote to these groups before the app supports their score rules.
  A bad first contact is hard to fix.

### People who are not the target now

- Mac users and phone-only users. Collect their emails or requests. They are
  the users of the macOS version (B-13).
- Professional teams and analysts. They need different tools.

## 7. How to get the first 10 users

A "user" here is a person who installs the app and exports at least one
video. A download alone is not a user. The cockpit shows both numbers.

A rough funnel (estimate): from strangers, 100 site visits give about 5 to 10
downloads, and about half of the downloads give an export. From a personal
request, the rate is much higher. Thus personal requests are the fastest way
to the first 10.

### Tactic 1: people in your own videos

- The players in your doubles and singles videos (Batumi) already saw the
  result. Ask each of them to record their next match and try the app.
- Send your opponents the edited video of your match together. People share
  videos where they play.
- Expected: 2 to 4 users.

### Tactic 2: your tennis friends and club

- Write to each tennis contact one by one. A group message is easy to
  ignore.
- Offer to sit with them for 10 minutes for the first project.
- Ask for one honest sentence of feedback after the first export.
- Expected: 2 to 4 users.

### Tactic 3: edit a match for a YouTuber (group B)

- Find 20 small channels that post full amateur matches with no score.
- Take one of their videos (ask first, or use a video with a permissive
  license). Edit it with BananaShot. Send the result to the owner with the
  template in section 9.
- Ask for nothing except to try the app for the next match.
- Expected: 3 to 5 replies, 1 to 3 users. Each user then shows the credit
  line to their viewers.

### Tactic 4: one honest Reddit post on r/10s

- Before the post: take part in r/10s for 1 to 2 weeks. Answer questions
  about recording and gear.
- The post: your story, one 30-second clip or a link to your match video,
  what the app does, that it is free, that it is Windows only. Ask for
  feedback, not for downloads.
- Expected: 50 to 500 visits, 1 to 5 users (high variation).

### Tactic 5: Talk Tennis forum

- Same approach as Reddit. Use the section for videos or general topics.
- Also reply to threads where people post raw match videos: "Here is a
  version with only the points, if it helps."

### Tactic 6: answer old questions

- Search for questions about tennis video editing on Reddit, forums, and
  Quora. Many have no good answer.
- Write a real answer with steps. Mention the app at the end. Say that you
  are the author.
- Expected: few users now, but the links work for years.

### Tactic 7: local chats in your languages

- Russian-speaking tennis chats (Telegram, WhatsApp) are large and active.
- Post only where you are a member and where the rules allow it. Post a
  video first, the link second.
- Expected: 1 to 3 users.

### Tactic 8: offline in Georgia

- Talk to 2 or 3 coaches at your club and at clubs in Batumi and Tbilisi.
  Show the app on a laptop with a video of their own player.
- Offer to edit the final of a local amateur tournament for free. The
  organizer posts it, and all players see it.
- Print a small card or a QR code to the site for the club notice board.
- Expected: 1 to 3 users, and good local content for your channel.

### Tactic 9: your 3-minute tutorial video

- Record your screen while you edit a short match. Show the four steps.
- Use it in every message above. A video answers most questions before
  people ask.

### A realistic plan for the first 10

| Tactic | Expected users |
|---|---|
| 1. People in your videos | 2 to 4 |
| 2. Tennis friends and club | 2 to 4 |
| 3. Edit for YouTubers | 1 to 3 |
| 4 and 5. Reddit and Talk Tennis | 1 to 5 |
| 7 and 8. Local chats and offline | 1 to 4 |
| Total | 7 to 20 |

Tactics 1 and 2 alone can give 5 users in one or two weeks.

## 10. What not to do

- Do not post the same text in many places on the same day. Moderators and
  spam filters see it.
- Do not post links in comments under other people's videos.
- Do not hide that you are the author. Honesty is the main advantage of a
  one-person project.
- Do not buy ads, followers, or reviews.
- Do not open many social accounts now. Each account is a promise to answer
  there (B-34).
- Do not launch on Product Hunt before you have a tutorial video and real
  user quotes.
- Do not promote to padel or pickleball players before the app supports
  their score rules.

- A collaboration with a tennis gear reviewer: give them a ready video of
  their own match.
- A score import from watch apps (Garmin, Apple Watch). Players who keep
  score on the watch then need only to mark the points.
