How to cut the dead time from a tennis match video

By Dmitrii Litvin, the author of BananaShot. Updated on 2026-10-18.

Most of a tennis recording is dead time: picking up balls, changeovers, and the walk back to the baseline. To cut it, you mark where each point starts and ends, and export only those parts. A two-hour match often becomes a 30-minute video.

Two ways to do it

1. By hand in a video editor

Any video editor (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro, CapCut, Shotcut, and others) can cut a video. Split the clip at the start and the end of each point, and delete the parts between them.

  • Good: you can use an editor that you already know, and you can cut anything.
  • Hard: a match often has more than 100 points, so you make 200 or more cuts. The editor doesn't know which part is a point, so a change later means more cuts by hand.

2. In a tennis video editor

An app for match videos lets you mark each point while you watch. It then exports only the points. BananaShot works like this. Some other apps use AI to find the points; then check the result, because the AI can cut a point too early or keep a ball pickup.

  • Good: one key marks the start, and one click ends the point. You can change any point later, and the export always uses your latest marks. The same marks also give you the score and the statistics. Video from any camera and any angle works, because the app doesn't need to see the court in a certain way.
  • Hard: you go through the match once. You can play the video faster between the points.

Step by step with BananaShot

BananaShot is a free app for Windows 10 and 11. It edits on your PC, and your video stays there.

  1. Open your recording. The app opens MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, M4V, and WMV files, including 4K 60fps. It only reads the file and never changes it.
  2. Mark the start of a point. Play the video. Just before the serve, click Point start or press one key.
  3. End the point. When the point is over, click the player who won it. If you don't want a score, click No score. This ends the point, and it shows in the list and on the timeline.
  4. Go on to the next point. Repeat until the end of the match. You can change, delete, or mark any point as a favorite in the list.
  5. Export only the points. On the Export tab, select Only points. The card shows how long the video will be. You can also add the scoreboard, your comments, and a statistics card.

Want to see it first? The 8-minute tutorial on YouTube (opens in a new tab) shows all of these steps with a real match.

Tips

  • Set the start a moment before the toss, and end the point a moment after the last shot. A short gap makes the cuts feel natural.
  • Play the video faster between the points, and slow it down for the end of a point.
  • Mark the best points as favorites while you go. Then you can export a highlights video too, with no extra work. See how to make a highlights video.
  • If the light changes or the phone moves during the match, you can correct the colors and the framing for that part of the video.
