How to record a tennis match with a phone

By Dmitrii Litvin, the author of BananaShot. Updated on 2026-10-18.

Put the phone behind the baseline, as high as you can, in the middle of the court. Record in landscape, with the whole court in the picture. Bring a power bank, and make sure that the phone has enough free storage for the whole match.

I record my own matches with a phone, on the back fence or on a tripod behind the baseline. This page is what works for me.

Where to put the phone

  • Behind the baseline, in the middle. From there, you see both players, the whole court, and where each ball lands.
  • High. Higher is better: the far half of the court gets bigger, and the net hides less. A fence clamp above head height or a tall tripod works well.
  • Safe from the ball. A ball hits the back fence often. Put the phone where a ball can't knock it down, or use a mount that holds it tight.
  • Out of the sun. A phone in direct sun can get too hot and stop the recording. Use some shade if you can.

Camera settings

  • Landscape. Turn the phone sideways before you start.
  • The main lens. Use the normal lens (1x). Use the wide lens only if the whole court doesn't fit.
  • Resolution and frame rate. 1080p is enough to watch the match. 4K lets you zoom and crop later without a blurry picture. 60fps makes fast shots smoother.
  • Check the picture. Before the first point, look at the screen: both baselines, both sidelines, and the net must be in the picture.

Battery and storage

  • Storage. A two-hour match in 4K 60fps can take about 50 GB. In 1080p, it's much less. Check the free space before the match.
  • Battery. Two hours of video use a lot of battery. Connect a power bank before you start.
  • No calls. Turn on airplane mode or "do not disturb", so that a call or an alarm doesn't stop the recording.

After the match

  • Copy the file to your PC. A USB cable is usually the fastest way for a big file.
  • Fix the picture. Is the video too dark, or is the phone not level? BananaShot can correct the colors, rotate the video, and crop it. If the light changes or the phone moves during the match, you can add a change point, and the video blends to the new values.
  • Cut and score it. See how to cut the dead time and how to add a scoreboard. You can also watch the 8-minute tutorial on YouTube (opens in a new tab).
