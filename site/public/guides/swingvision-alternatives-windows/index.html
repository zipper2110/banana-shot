SwingVision alternatives for Windows

By Dmitrii Litvin, the author of BananaShot. Updated on 2026-10-18.

SwingVision runs only on Apple devices: iPhone, iPad, and Mac. On a Windows PC, you can use BananaShot (free, for video from any camera, with a scoreboard and statistics), Rallytics (finds the rallies with AI), Tennis Studio (a score overlay from a Garmin watch app), or a general video editor. Each one is good at something different.

I tried Rallytics and Tennis Studio myself in October 2026, and I checked the facts about the other apps on their own pages on 2026-10-18. Apps change, so check them before you buy.

What SwingVision does well

SwingVision records the match with an iPhone or iPad and uses AI to call the lines, count the score, and track the shots. That's something no Windows app on this page does. The app is free to download. SwingVision Pro, with the automated scoring and the line calls, is a subscription: $179.99 per year on the US App Store on the date of the check.

For the AI to work, the iPhone or iPad itself records the match, from a set place: behind the baseline, high enough to show both baselines and the alleys. When I tried it, the app also asked for a set zoom on the court. Many AI apps need a camera view like this.

If you record with an Android phone, an action camera, or from another angle, or you want to edit on your PC, the apps below are the options.

The Windows options

BananaShot

A free tennis and padel match video editor for Windows 10 and 11. You mark each point and click the player who won it. The app cuts the dead time, counts the score by the rules, and adds a scoreboard, match statistics, and comments to the video. All work happens on your PC, with no upload.

Any video works. You can record with any phone, an action camera, or a video camera, in any resolution. The angle, the light, and the zoom don't matter, because you mark the points and the app doesn't need to see the court in a certain way.

  • Better at: videos from any camera and any angle, a correct score and a scoreboard in 19 styles, statistics, color correction, rotation and crop, a flexible export, a YouTube thumbnail, and no cost. A 4K 60fps match of 50 GB opens in about a second.
  • Not for you if: you want the points found for you. You go through the match once and mark them yourself.

Rallytics

A desktop app for Windows and Mac. It uses AI to find each rally and cuts the gaps between them. It sends a low-resolution copy of the video for the analysis and cuts the original file on your computer. You can correct the rallies in its editor. It has only the cut: there's no score, no scoreboard, and no color correction or crop.

  • Better at: finding the rallies with no clicks from you.
  • In my test: the app froze often, and I found the interface hard to use. To fix a rally takes more steps than to mark a point in BananaShot.
  • Price: 2 hours of analysis free, then one-time packs from $4.99 for 3 hours, or $89.99 for a year (8 hours per month), on rallytics.app on the date of the check.

Tennis Studio

A basic Windows app in the Microsoft Store. It can cut, crop, and rotate match videos, and show a score overlay. The scores come from the Tennis Studio app for Garmin watches, or you add them by hand.

  • Better at: a score overlay if you already keep score on a Garmin watch.
  • In my test: you can't change the style of the scoreboard, and the app has no color correction. Marking and changing points is slower than in BananaShot.
  • Price: it cost me $8 in the Microsoft Store. The price can be different in your country.

In my test, neither app had an export as flexible as BananaShot's (the full match, only the points, or only the favorites, with your choice of quality and a statistics card), and neither can make a YouTube thumbnail.

A general video editor

DaVinci Resolve and Shotcut are free video editors for Windows. You can cut each point by hand and type the score in a text layer.

  • Better at: full control of every frame, and any other video work.
  • Not for you if: you don't want to make 200 or more cuts and type the score after each point. See how to cut the dead time.

Which one to choose

  • You want the score on the video and the statistics: BananaShot.
  • You record with an Android phone, an action camera, or from the side of the court: BananaShot.
  • You want only the rallies, and no clicks: Rallytics.
  • You keep score on a Garmin watch: Tennis Studio.
  • You edit other videos too and have time: a general video editor.

You can also use two of them together. For example, cut the rallies in one app, and use BananaShot for a full match with the score.
