How to add a scoreboard to a tennis video

By Dmitrii Litvin, the author of BananaShot. Updated on 2026-10-18.

A scoreboard needs the score after each point and a graphic that changes with it. You can make it by hand in a video editor, keep score in a phone app while you record, or score the recording after the match in an app made for this. For a full match that you already recorded, the last way is usually the fastest.

Three ways to do it

1. By hand in a video editor

Any video editor (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro, CapCut, Shotcut, and others) can show text over the video. Make a text layer with the score, and change it after each point.

  • Good: you have full control of the look, and you probably already know the editor.
  • Hard: a match often has more than 100 points. Each point needs a new score in the layer, and the editor doesn't know the tennis rules. A wrong score is easy to type and hard to find.

2. In a phone app while you record

Some phone apps record the video and let you tap the score during the match. The score is in the video at once.

  • Good: there's no work after the match.
  • Hard: somebody must keep score during the match, and that's not easy when you're the one who plays. The video usually keeps all the time between the points.

3. After the match, in a tennis video editor

Record the match with any camera. After the match, an app for match videos lets you mark the points and give each point its winner. The app counts the score by the tennis rules and draws the scoreboard. BananaShot works like this. Some other apps use AI to find the points; then check the result, because a wrong point changes the score.

  • Good: you can record with any camera, and you can fix any point later. The app can also cut the dead time between the points.
  • Hard: you need some time after the match. With BananaShot, you go through the match once and click the winner of each point.

Step by step with BananaShot

BananaShot is a free app for Windows 10 and 11. It edits on your PC, and your video stays there. Big files are not a problem: a 4K 60fps recording of a full match (about 50 GB) opens in about a second, because there's nothing to upload.

  1. Open your recording. The app opens MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, M4V, and WMV files, including 4K 60fps. When you score the first point, the app asks for the player names and colors and the match format.
  2. Mark the points. Play the video. At the start of a point, set the start with one key. At the end of the point, click the player who won it. This ends the point, and the score on the video changes.
  3. Choose the look. Click Scoreboard style. You can choose from 19 styles and put the scoreboard in any corner of the video. You can also change its size, background, and accent color, and add a title, for example the name of your club or tournament.
  4. Check the score. If the match started from a score other than zero, or a point is wrong, select the point and click Override score. The points after it count from the new score.
  5. Export the video. On the Export tab, select Scoreboard. You can export the full match, only the points, or a highlights video of your favorite points. The app writes a new MP4 file and doesn't change your recording.

Want to see it first? The 8-minute tutorial on YouTube (opens in a new tab) shows all of these steps with a real match.

Tips for a good scoreboard

  • Put the scoreboard in a corner where it doesn't cover the court. With a camera behind the baseline, a top corner often shows only the fence or the sky.
  • Mark the server of one point. The app then follows the serve for the whole match and shows a ball next to the server.
  • The app can count best of 1, 3, or 5 sets, a match tiebreak instead of the last set, a pro set, games only, and plain points. You can also select the deuce rule.
  • For padel, the scoreboard can show a badge during a golden point or a star point.
  • New projects start with your last scoreboard style, so you choose the look only once.
